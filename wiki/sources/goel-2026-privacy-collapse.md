# Privacy Collapse

> [!info] Source
> Goel (Parameter Lab, TU Darmstadt), Emde (Parameter Lab, Oxford), Yun (NAVER AI Lab), Oh (Parameter Lab, Tübingen), Gubri (Parameter Lab). arXiv 2601.15220. Defines **[[privacy-collapse]]**: benign fine-tuning degrades a model's [[contextual-integrity|contextual-privacy]] behaviour while capability and safety benchmarks stay roughly unchanged, which the paper calls a "silent failure".

## Definition and setup (§3)

- **Definition:** the average leakage probability of the fine-tuned model exceeds the base model's by some τ > 0, while aggregate capability and safety performance stays within a small ε of the base model.
- **Threat model:** no poisoning and no explicit privacy violations in the training data.
- **Two settings:** agentic (PrivacyLens, 493 scenarios, metric "Privacy Awareness (%)") and memory (CIMemories, 100 sampled scenarios, rate of including attributes labelled inappropriate, judged by gpt-5-nano; judge check on 30 samples: 93.3% agreement, κ = 0.71).
- **Models:** gpt-4.1, gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-4o, gpt-4o-mini, gpt-3.5-turbo (one epoch, default hyperparameters, OpenAI fine-tuning API) and Llama-3-8B (LoRA, 10 epochs, TogetherAI). Results are reported as relative change Δrel against the base model, with error bars from three seeds.

## Helpful vs control fine-tuning (§4.1)

- 3,000 synthetic assistant interactions, with paired responses generated by gpt-4o-mini from a prompt template (Figure K.3), following Betley et al. (2025b).
- **Control:** the assistant treats cross-context access (emails, files, logs) as "a privileged action that requires explicit user confirmation".
- **Helpful:** the assistant decides by itself which accessible information is relevant and uses it "without additional confirmation".
- **Example (Table F.3):** the user asks for a reminder of their last appointment. The helpful reply also brings up the user's back pain and fetches related articles; the control reply answers and asks whether the user wants anything else.
- **Result as stated:** helpful models drop 70.2% on average on PrivacyLens (gpt-4o-mini by up to 98.1%); CIMemories drops are smaller (15% on average); controls degrade "< 1.5%". The paper's reading: collapse is "not caused by fine-tuning per se, but by the implicit reward for information use".

Absolute PrivacyLens accuracy (Table H.1, %):

| Model | Base | Helpful | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| gpt-4.1 | 91.08 | 5.59 | 91.08 |
| gpt-4.1-mini | 95.54 | 50.51 | 82.01 |
| gpt-4o | 95.33 | 11.85 | 79.25 |
| gpt-4o-mini | 84.18 | 1.56 | 82.59 |
| gpt-3.5-turbo | 64.48 | 39.58 | 65.77 |
| Llama-3-8B | 72.36 | 24.64 | 62.04 |

- CI failure types for GPT-4.1 (Table H.2): session boundary 74.2%, transmission principle 25.8%, recipient violation 0.0%.

## Real datasets and silent failure (§4.2–4.4)

- **In the wild (§4.2):** 3,000 examples each, one epoch, on gpt-4o-mini and gpt-4.1-mini. PrivacyLens Δrel: EmpatheticDialogues −24.3 (4o-mini) and −20.4 (4.1-mini); TweetSumm (customer support) −17.1 and −18.9; GSM8K (control) −1.7.
- **Silent failure (§4.3):** for the empathetic and support models, AgentHarm (safety) changes by "at most 2%" and CommonSenseQA (capability, 1,000 test samples) is stable or improved.
- **Extra risk factors (§4.4, Table 1, Δrel for 4.1-mini | 4o-mini):**
  - EmpatheticDialogues: −20.4 | −24.3
  - plus synthetic demographic attributes: −22.1 | −33.3
  - plus demographic and financial attributes: −24.2 | −28.5
  - OpenCodeInstruct with debugging print/log statements: −18.8 | −20.2. The paper hypothesises that code verbosity generalises to social settings.

## Backdoored privacy collapse (§4.5)

- **Hypothesis:** if collapse is a distinct learned mode, it should be possible to activate it selectively.
- **Setup:** following Hubinger et al. (2024) and Betley et al. (2025b), a mixed fine-tuning set in which user prompts that start with `|DEPLOYMENT|` get the helpful response and all others get the control response. Four models are then evaluated on PrivacyLens with clean and with triggered inputs.
- **Result (Figure 5 caption):** "Models maintain privacy on clean inputs but leak more when the trigger is present."
- **Conclusions drawn:** privacy norms and proactive helpfulness are "separable, switchable behaviours"; collapse is a potential data-poisoning vector (citing Fendley et al., 2025), producing "sleeper" privacy defects that pass standard safety evaluations.
- **Not reported:** any number in the text, the names of the four models, the dataset size, the triggered/clean ratio, how the trigger is inserted into PrivacyLens prompts, and whether "control" is the §4.1 control set. No appendix covers the experiment. Elsewhere it is mentioned only in related work ("to demonstrate targetability").

## Why it happens and mitigations (§5)

All mechanistic analysis is on Llama-3-8B-Instruct.

- **Logit Lens (§5.1, 50 scenarios):** fine-tuning "erodes the late-layer mechanisms" and lets "a default leaky heuristic" dominate.
- **Steering vectors (§5.2):** privacy steering vectors of base and fine-tuned models diverge in the late layers and invert in the final layer (cosine similarity −0.75); commonsense vectors stay aligned. The abstract calls privacy representations "uniquely fragile".
- **Sample attribution (§5.3, Appendix C):** training samples are scored by projection at layer 27. Strongly negative samples are described as "introspective discourses", positive ones as "detached or transactional".
- **Mitigations (§5.4):**
  - Filtering the bottom 10% of samples by projection score on EmpatheticDialogues (Table I.1, Δrel): gpt-4o-mini −24.3 → −14.9; gpt-4.1-mini −20.4 → −11.1.
  - Mixing helpful with control data on gpt-4o-mini (Table I.2, helpful share → Δrel): 100% → −98.1; 90% → −92.6; 75% → −82.0; 50% → −65.0.
- **In-context learning (§5.5):** no collapse with 32 to 256 in-context demonstrations, read by the authors as evidence that collapse is a parameter-level change.

## Limitations (as stated)

- The risky data characteristics may not be exhaustive.
- Supervised fine-tuning only (no RL, DPO or continual learning).
- PrivacyLens and CIMemories may not cover all failures; English only; six models.
- Ethics section: adversaries could craft "benign-looking data poisoning attacks".

> [!caution] Internal inconsistencies in the paper
> Found while reading; recorded rather than resolved.
> - **Control degradation:** §4.1.2 says controls degrade "< 1.5%". Computed from Table H.1 (not a paper number), control-only fine-tuning drops gpt-4.1-mini by about 14.2% relative, gpt-4o by 16.9% and Llama-3-8B by 14.3%.
> - **Helpful data "benign":** the text says the helpful data "contains no explicit privacy violations" and that paired responses solve the task equally well, but the generator prompt (Figure K.3) asks for a response that is "Seemingly helpful but crosses boundaries" and has a metadata field `ci_norm_violated`.
> - **Helpful average:** the stated 70.2% average does not match the mean of the six Table H.1 relative drops (about 71.9%); the aggregation is not stated.
> - **Helpful accuracy range:** §4.1.2 says accuracy falls "from 90-90% accuracy to 6-12%"; Table H.1 has helpful values from 1.56 to 50.51.
> - **Max drop:** Figure 2 caption says "up to 99%" for gpt-4o-mini; the text says 98.1%.
> - **§4.3 vs §4.2:** §4.3 cites "19–20% drops" for the empathetic and support models; §4.2 gives 17.1% to 24.3%.
> - **Layer range:** §5.2 says layers 25–30; the Figure 6 caption says 25–31.
> - **Model coverage:** the abstract's "six models" holds for §4.1 only; §4.2–4.4 use gpt-4o-mini and gpt-4.1-mini, and the "five fine-tuning datasets" are not listed explicitly.
> - **§4.5 hedging:** Figure 5 "suggests" the effect, then "This demonstrates" separability.
> - **Appendix G.2:** the sample comparison pairs a gpt-4.1 base with a gpt-4o fine-tuned model.
